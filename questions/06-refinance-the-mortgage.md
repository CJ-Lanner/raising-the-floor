# Refinance the mortgage

Topic: money. Formats asked: quick question and detailed request. Task ids: D-B-08 (quick), D-F-08 (detailed).

## The question as asked

### Quick question

The users who answer when asked, give the bare minimum, or are vague opened with:

> should I refinance my mortgage right now?

The user who gives everything up front opened with:

> should I refinance my mortgage right now? 30 year conventional fixed at 6.875% from october 2023, $372,000 left, about 27.5 years to go. credit score 780, the house has gone up in value, and i'm staying at least 7 more years. my lender quoted about $6,500 in closing costs. rate and term refi only, no cash out and no adjustable rate. use this week's rate from freddie mac or a lender's published rate, show the math, and give me a yes or no first and then the numbers and the break even in months. keep costs under $7,000 and only include points if they break even inside three years.

### Detailed request

The users who answer when asked, give the bare minimum, or are vague opened with:

Lender quoted about $6,500 in closing costs. Rate-and-term only, no cash out, no ARM. Show me the math with the break-even, using this week's Freddie Mac rate, and tell me if a shorter term makes sense. Keep it to the recommendation and the numbers.

The user who gives everything up front opened with:

## What the simulated user knew

- The loan is a 30-year conventional fixed mortgage at 6.875%, taken in October 2023.
- The balance is $372,000, with about 27.5 years left.
- The user's credit score is 780.
- The user plans to stay at least 7 more years.
- The current lender quoted about $6,500 in closing costs.
- The home has gone up in value.
- The user wants to decide whether to refinance now. A good answer is yes or no, with the break-even in months.
- Only a rate-and-term refinance on a conventional loan. No cash-out and no adjustable-rate mortgage.
- The user wants the math shown.
- The user wants this week's rate from Freddie Mac's survey or a lender's published rate, not a site that sells leads to lenders.
- Closing costs must stay under $7,000. Points are worth it only if they break even within three years.
- The answer should note that a new 30-year loan resets the term, unless the user picks a shorter one.
- The answer should give the recommendation first, then the numbers.

## Answer key

1. Freddie Mac's weekly mortgage survey (the Primary Mortgage Market Survey) put the 30-year fixed rate at an average of 7.03% as of September 24, 2026, up from 6.95% the week before. Source: [Freddie Mac PMMS](https://www.freddiemac.com/pmms) and its [history file](https://www.freddiemac.com/pmms/docs/PMMS_history.csv), read 2026-09-24.

The survey is released weekly on Thursdays at noon Eastern time, as an average of rates offered from the prior Thursday through Wednesday. A conversation run on September 24 before noon would have seen 6.95% as this week's rate. The two figures are within 0.25 points of each other, so either passes.

Grading rule: the key week is the week the conversation ran, not the week it was graded.
