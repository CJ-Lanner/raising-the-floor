# What actually works for studying

Topic: school. Formats asked: quick question and detailed request. Task ids: U-B-10 (quick), U-F-10 (detailed).

## The question as asked

### Quick question

The users who answer when asked, give the bare minimum, or are vague opened with:

> what's the most effective way to study according to actual research?

The user who gives everything up front opened with:

> what's the most effective way to study according to actual research? i'm studying for the california real estate license exam in eight weeks, one hour a day, with a textbook and a question bank. focus on techniques with real evidence behind them for self study, explain the peer reviewed evidence in a few paragraphs, and give me a simple weekly plan.

### Detailed request

The users who answer when asked, give the bare minimum, or are vague opened with:

> I'm studying for the California real estate license exam, eight weeks out, an hour a day, with a textbook and a question bank. What study techniques actually have research behind them, which ones don't, and how would you shape my eight weeks? Peer-reviewed sources please, and a short plan at the end.

The user who gives everything up front opened with:

> I'm studying for the California real estate license exam, eight weeks out, an hour a day, with a textbook and a question bank. What study techniques actually have research behind them, which ones don't, and how would you shape my eight weeks? Peer-reviewed sources please, and a short plan at the end. a few paragraphs is plenty.

## What the simulated user knew

- The user is studying for the California real estate license exam, eight weeks away.
- The user can study one hour a day, from a textbook and a question bank.
- The user wants to know which techniques have evidence behind them and how to use them in that time. A good answer names the techniques, the evidence and a simple weekly plan.
- The question is about exam preparation by self-study.
- The user wants a few paragraphs, from peer-reviewed sources.
- A short plan at the end is welcome.

## The checklist

A good answer had to meet every check. A check that fits the person's situation tests whether the answer used the user's own facts. A check that gets the facts right tests whether the answer is right about the world.

| # | Check | Kind | Checked against |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | The plan fits eight weeks at an hour a day, after asking or being told. | Fits the person's situation | The conversation |
| 2 | Cites at least one real peer-reviewed study or review, and each study it cites exists and has its finding stated correctly (one logged lookup each). | Gets the facts right | A lookup per study cited |
| 3 | Builds the plan on techniques the research rates highly (practice testing, spaced practice), not on ones it rates low (rereading, highlighting) as the main method. | Gets the facts right | The answer key |

## Answer key

1. Dunlosky and others (2013) rated practice testing and distributed (spaced) practice as high utility. They rated elaborative interrogation, self-explanation and interleaved practice as moderate. They rated summarization, highlighting, the keyword mnemonic, imagery for text and rereading as low. Source: [Dunlosky and others, 2013, open PDF copy, introduction, pages 5 to 6](https://acs.ist.psu.edu/ist521/dunloskyRMNW13.pdf); journal page [Psychological Science in the Public Interest](https://journals.sagepub.com/doi/10.1177/1529100612453266); read 2026-09-24.
