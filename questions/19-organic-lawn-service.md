# An organic lawn service

Topic: home. Formats asked: quick question and detailed request. Task ids: D-B-04 (quick), D-F-04 (detailed).

## The question as asked

### Quick question

The users who answer when asked, give the bare minimum, or are vague opened with:

> Can you find me a lawn care company that does organic?

The user who gives everything up front opened with:

> Can you find me a lawn care company that does organic? i'm in atlanta on a quarter acre lot. i want ongoing fertilizing and weed control, not mowing, from a company that's actually operating now and whose whole program is free of synthetic fertilizer, pesticide and herbicide. 2 or 3 companies that serve atlanta, what each program includes, and what google, yelp or bbb say about them. check what the program includes on the company's own site, not on those directory sites that just pass your info to contractors. one line each. around $100 or less per treatment.

### Detailed request

The users who answer when asked, give the bare minimum, or are vague opened with:

Quarter acre, fertilizing and weed control only, I mow myself. Up to about $100 a treatment. Can you give me two or three companies with what their program actually includes, based on their own websites? Skip the lead-gen directories.

The user who gives everything up front opened with:

Quarter acre, fertilizing and weed control only, I mow myself. Up to about $100 a treatment. ## What the simulated user knew

- The user owns a home in Atlanta, Georgia, on a quarter-acre lot.
- The user wants ongoing fertilizing and weed control, not mowing.
- The user wants to hire one company whose program is genuinely organic. A good answer is two or three named companies that actually offer such a program, with what each includes.
- Only companies that serve Atlanta and are operating now. No do-it-yourself product advice and no mowing-only services.
- The user wants enough to check each company's program, not a report.
- The user wants each company's own site for what the program contains, and Google, Yelp or BBB for reputation. Directory sites that sell the user's details to contractors should be flagged.
- The budget is up to about $100 per treatment.
- "Organic" means no synthetic fertilizer and no synthetic pesticide or herbicide across the whole program. "Eco-friendly", "natural", or organic as one add-on does not count.
- The answer should be a short list, one line per company.

## Answer key

The key lists known Atlanta candidates.

1. Simply Organic Turf Care (Norcross, Georgia; a division of Senske) runs an eight-application Atlanta program with a 100% organic option. Its default hybrid option uses synthetic herbicides. Applications 3, 4 and 8 list "Targeted weed control" without naming the product. No per-treatment prices are published. Source: [Simply Organic Turf Care, Atlanta program options](https://simplyorganicturfcare.com/program-options/simply-organic-lawn-care-in-atlanta/), read 2026-09-24.
2. Clean Air Lawn Care's Atlanta page says it serves Atlanta and describes "organic" fertilizing and "safer" weed control. Its own pages never say the whole program is free of synthetic inputs. They say "harsh" chemicals are avoided. Sources: [Clean Air Lawn Care, Atlanta](https://cleanairlawncare.com/service-locations/ga/atlanta/) and [Clean Air Lawn Care, natural weed control](https://cleanairlawncare.com/services/natural-weed-control/), read 2026-09-24.

Grading rules:

- Simply Organic Turf Care counts as a candidate only if the reply names its 100% organic option and says the user must confirm with the company that no synthetic herbicide or pre-emergent is used. The reason is the company's own FAQ, which says "there's no such thing as 100% organic turf care" and that its eight-step program applies a non-organic pre-emergent twice a year and targeted synthetic herbicide as needed ([Simply Organic Turf Care FAQ](https://simplyorganicturfcare.com/faqs/), read 2026-09-28). A reply that rules the company out on that FAQ is not wrong.
- Clean Air Lawn Care counts as a candidate only if the reply says the user must call to confirm the program uses no synthetic inputs, because the company's pages do not say so.
- The Green Queen counts as a candidate only if the reply names its Full Organic program and says the user must confirm that its iron-based weed spray contains no synthetic ingredients. Its [turf page](https://thegreenqueen.com/services/turf-and-lawn/) says the Full Organic program "uses only registered organic and natural products", and its [FAQ](https://thegreenqueen.com/faq/) lists iron-based weed sprays listed by OMRI, a body that reviews products for organic use. An OMRI listing does not on its own show a product is non-synthetic. A reply that rules the company out on that spray is not wrong, and a reply that does not find the company does not fail for that alone.
