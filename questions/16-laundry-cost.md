# Cost of a load of laundry at home

Topic: home. Formats asked: quick question and detailed request. Task ids: F-B-05 (quick), F-F-05 (detailed).

## The question as asked

### Quick question

The users who answer when asked, give the bare minimum, or are vague opened with:

> how much does it cost to do a load of laundry at home?

The user who gives everything up front opened with:

> how much does it cost to do a load of laundry at home? denver apartment on xcel with a standard top loader and electric dryer in the unit, and i pay my own water and sewer. about five loads a week, mostly on cold. a short per load estimate at current rates, split into water, sewer, electricity and detergent.

### Detailed request

All four simulated users opened with the same message:

> I'm in a Denver apartment on Xcel with a standard top-loader and electric dryer in the unit, and I pay my own water and sewer. I do about five loads a week, mostly on cold. Roughly what does one load cost me in water, sewer, electricity and detergent at current rates? Use Xcel's and Denver's rates if you can and just show me the pieces.

## What the simulated user knew

- The user lives in a Denver apartment, with electricity from Xcel Energy.
- The unit has a standard top-loading washer and an electric dryer.
- The user pays their own water and sewer.
- The user does about five loads a week, mostly cold washes.
- The user wants the running cost of one load, split into water, sewer, electricity and detergent. A good answer is one number per load with the pieces shown.
- The user wants a short estimate at current rates, using Xcel's rates if possible.

## Answer key

1. Xcel Colorado base energy prices per kWh (summer is June to September, winter is October to May). Flat rate (R-OO): $0.10380 summer, $0.08570 winter. Time of use (RE-TOU): on-peak (weekdays 5 to 9 pm) $0.21277 summer and $0.18331 winter; off-peak $0.07884 summer and $0.06792 winter. The bill adds a $7.10 monthly charge, riders, fees and taxes. Source: [Xcel Energy, time-of-use pricing](https://co.my.xcelenergy.com/s/billing-payment/residential-rates/time-of-use-pricing), read 2026-09-24. Winter prices start October 1, and the run spanned that date, so both seasons are in the range.
2. Xcel Colorado all-in energy prices per kWh, in effect from August 29, 2026, before franchise fees and taxes. The riders on base energy add 57.73% in all, plus $0.01452 per kWh, plus a small summer charge, then 3.25% on the subtotal. All-in prices: RE-TOU winter off-peak $0.12560, summer off-peak $0.14338, winter on-peak $0.31352, summer on-peak $0.36220; R-OO winter $0.15456, summer $0.18417. The riders add 70% to 85% to the base price. Source: Xcel's "Summary of Electric Rates as of 09-01-26", linked from the "Summary of Electric Rates" tab of [Xcel Energy's rate books](https://www.xcelenergy.com/company/rates_and_regulations/rates/rate_books), read 2026-09-25. One rider resets each quarter, and a new one took effect on October 1, 2026.
3. Denver Water 2026, inside the City of Denver, single-family residential Tier 1: $3.02 per 1,000 gallons, with no drought charge. Source: [Denver Water 2026 residential rates](https://www.denverwater.org/residential/billing-and-rates/2026-rates), read 2026-09-24.
4. Denver Water bills multifamily properties, including duplexes to 5-plexes, on business rates. The 2026 nonresidential Tier 1 price inside Denver is $3.55 per 1,000 gallons. Source: [Denver Water 2026 business rates](https://www.denverwater.org/business/billing-and-rates/2026-rates), read 2026-09-25.
5. Denver's 2026 residential sanitary sewer charge is $5.88 per 1,000 gallons, with a minimum of $16.06 a month per living unit, billed on the Denver Water bill. For residential customers, February's water use sets the sewer charge for the whole year from March. Source: [City of Denver, wastewater billing](https://www.denvergov.org/My-Property/Utilities/Wastewater-Billing-Support), read 2026-09-25.
6. An ENERGY STAR full-size washer uses 14 gallons a load. A standard machine uses 20. Source: [ENERGY STAR, clothes washers](https://www.energystar.gov/products/clothes_washers), read 2026-09-24.
7. The ENERGY STAR Version 1.1 minimum for a standard electric dryer is a Combined Energy Factor of 3.93 lb per kWh (pounds of laundry dried per kWh). Source: [ENERGY STAR Version 2.0 Clothes Dryer Discussion Guide, March 2024, Table 1, page 4](https://www.energystar.gov/sites/default/files/ENERGY%20STAR%20Version%202.0%20Clothes%20Dryer%20Discussion%20Guide.pdf), read 2026-09-24.
8. The federal test load for a standard-size dryer is 8.45 lb, bone dry. Source: [10 CFR 430 Subpart B, Appendix D2, section 2.7.1](https://www.ecfr.gov/current/title-10/chapter-II/subchapter-D/part-430/subpart-B/appendix-Appendix%20D2%20to%20Subpart%20B%20of%20Part%20430), read 2026-09-24.
9. The federal minimum for a standard vented electric dryer made since 2015 is a Combined Energy Factor of 3.73 lb per kWh, below ENERGY STAR's 3.93. A dryer at that minimum uses about 2.27 kWh per test load. A new minimum of 3.93 applies to dryers made from March 1, 2028. Source: [10 CFR 430.32(h)(3)](https://www.ecfr.gov/current/title-10/chapter-II/subchapter-D/part-430/subpart-C/section-430.32), read 2026-09-24.
10. The range. The dryer uses 8.45 ÷ 3.93 = 2.15 kWh (ENERGY STAR) to 8.45 ÷ 3.73 = 2.27 kWh (federal minimum) per load. Electricity for the dryer, at the all-in prices in line 2: low 2.15 × $0.12560 = $0.27; high 2.27 × $0.36220 = $0.82; on the flat rate, 2.15 × $0.15456 = $0.33 to 2.27 × $0.18417 = $0.42; likeliest (off-peak or flat rate) $0.27 to $0.42. Water: 14 gallons × $3.02 per 1,000 = $0.04, to 20 gallons × $3.55 per 1,000 = $0.07. Sewer: 14 to 20 gallons × $5.88 per 1,000 = $0.08 to $0.12. Electricity, water and sewer together: $0.39 to $1.01 a load, likeliest about $0.39 to $0.61. Not in the key: the washer motor (no official per-load figure exists; ENERGY STAR says water heating is about 90% of a washer's energy), franchise fees and taxes, the $7.10 and $0.83 monthly charges, and detergent.

Grading rules:

- The grader adds the reply's electricity, water and sewer pieces and compares the sum with check 2's range ($0.30 to $1.25), which widens the total in line 10. The pieces are not graded one by one. The detergent figure is not checked.
- A reply that adds a small figure for the washer motor (a few tenths of a kWh or less) does not fail on that addition alone.
- A load's real extra sewer cost is $5.88 per 1,000 gallons only through February use. An apartment on the $16.06 minimum pays nothing extra per load. A reply passes if it uses $5.88 per 1,000 gallons, or if it explains the February basis.
