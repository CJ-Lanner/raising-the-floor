# Repair or replace the car

Topic: cars. Formats asked: quick question and detailed request. Task ids: D-B-06 (quick), D-F-06 (detailed).

## The question as asked

### Quick question

The users who answer when asked, give the bare minimum, or are vague opened with:

> Should I fix my car or just get a new one?

The user who gives everything up front opened with:

> Should I fix my car or just get a new one? 2016 honda civic lx, 148,000 miles, i own it outright. the transmission is going, it's the cvt automatic. dealer quoted $4,200, an independent shop quoted $3,300 for a rebuilt unit with a 12 month warranty. it runs fine otherwise, new tires last year, and i drive about 12,000 miles a year. compare the repair against what it's worth on kelley blue book or edmunds right now and what a reliable used replacement under $15,000 would get me, using current values and independent reliability sources. then give me a straight recommendation with the math in a few lines. i'm ok spending up to $3,500 on the repair and would rather not spend $15,000. not looking at leasing or new cars.

## What the simulated user knew

- The car is a 2016 Honda Civic LX with 148,000 miles, owned outright.
- The CVT automatic transmission is failing.
- The dealer quoted $4,200. An independent shop quoted $3,300 for a rebuilt unit with a 12-month warranty.
- The car otherwise runs fine, with new tires last year.
- The user drives about 12,000 miles a year.
- The user wants a clear recommendation with the numbers behind it, walked through in a few lines.
- The choice is a repair or a used replacement. Leasing and new cars are out.
- The user wants the car's current value from a recognized guide (KBB or Edmunds), and reliability from independent sources.
- A repair of up to $3,500 is fine. A replacement could go up to $15,000, but the user would rather not spend that.
- The user wants the repair compared with the car's current value and with what $15,000 buys, not a bare "50% rule" with no numbers.

## Answer key

1. Kelley Blue Book values a 2016 Honda Civic LX Sedan 4D at 148,000 miles, with default options and no VIN, as follows. Fair condition: $8,195 to $9,470 private party, and $5,535 to $6,985 trade-in. Good condition: $9,420 to $10,820 private party. Sources: KBB [fair, private party](https://www.kbb.com/honda/civic/2016/lx-sedan-4d/?condition=fair&intent=trade-in-sell&mileage=148000&pricetype=private-party), [fair, trade-in](https://www.kbb.com/honda/civic/2016/lx-sedan-4d/?condition=fair&intent=trade-in-sell&mileage=148000&pricetype=trade-in) and [good, private party](https://www.kbb.com/honda/civic/2016/lx-sedan-4d/?condition=good&intent=trade-in-sell&mileage=148000&pricetype=private-party), all read 2026-09-24. KBB values update weekly and vary by ZIP code, and the question gives no ZIP code.
2. KBB's overview page gives figures at its default mileage, not at 148,000 miles: about $8,020 to $9,845 trade-in and $10,580 to $12,880 private party. A reply that quotes those figures without adjusting for mileage is above the key range at the top. Source: [KBB 2016 Civic LX overview](https://www.kbb.com/honda/civic/2016/lx-sedan-4d/), read 2026-09-24.
3. The range for check 3: at fair condition, KBB trade-in low ($5,535) to KBB private party high ($9,470), about $5,500 to $9,500. Counting good condition, the top rises to $10,820.

Grading rule: the key uses KBB only. Edmunds' value for this car at 148,000 miles could not be read without filling in its appraisal form, so a reply that quotes an Edmunds figure gets the grader's own lookup.
