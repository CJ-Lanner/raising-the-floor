## Answer key

1. For plan years that start in 2026, the most an FSA can carry over is $680, set by Revenue Procedure 2025-32. Sources: [Rev. Proc. 2025-32, section 4.15, pages 18 to 19](https://www.irs.gov/pub/irs-drop/rp-25-32.pdf) and [IRS news release IR-2025-103, October 9, 2025](https://www.irs.gov/newsroom/irs-releases-tax-inflation-adjustments-for-tax-year-2026-including-amendments-from-the-one-big-beautiful-bill), read 2026-09-24. When read, Publication 969 on irs.gov was still the 2025 edition and said $660, the cap for 2025 plan years. Source: [IRS Publication 969](https://www.irs.gov/publications/p969), read 2026-09-24.
2. An HSA is portable. It stays with the user if they change employers or leave work, and contributions stay in the account until used. Source: [IRS Publication 969](https://www.irs.gov/publications/p969), "What are the benefits of an HSA?", read 2026-09-24.
3. An FSA is generally use-it-or-lose-it. The plan may offer either a grace period (up to 2½ months) or a carryover. Source: [IRS Publication 969](https://www.irs.gov/publications/p969), "Balance in an FSA", read 2026-09-24.
4. An HRA must be funded solely by the employer, not through the employee's salary. Source: [IRS Publication 969](https://www.irs.gov/publications/p969), "Health Reimbursement Arrangements (HRAs)", read 2026-09-24.
5. Some HRAs, but not all, let unused amounts carry to the next year. The employer cannot refund the balance. Source: [IRS Publication 969](https://www.irs.gov/publications/p969), "Balance in an HRA", read 2026-09-24.

Grading rule: replies are held to $680 for a 2026 plan year. A reply that says $660 passes only if it says $660 is the 2025 cap.
