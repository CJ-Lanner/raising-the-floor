# Latest research on melanoma treatment

Topic: health. Formats asked: quick question and detailed request. Task ids: U-B-12 (quick), U-F-12 (detailed).

## The question as asked

### Quick question

The users who answer when asked, give the bare minimum, or are vague opened with:

> what's the latest research on melanoma treatment?

The user who gives everything up front opened with:

> what's the latest research on melanoma treatment? my dad was diagnosed with stage iii melanoma last month. i'm not a clinician but i read well. i want a thorough readable overview of the current treatment options and what's changed in the last three years, from proper medical sources, the kind his oncologist would trust. no miracle cure sites.

### Detailed request

All four simulated users opened with the same message:

> My dad was diagnosed with stage III melanoma last month. I'm not a doctor but I read well, and I want to understand the current treatment options and what's changed in the last three years, immunotherapy, targeted therapy, adjuvant versus neoadjuvant, so I can follow his oncologist. Thorough but readable, proper medical sources only, the kind his oncologist would trust, no miracle-cure sites. I know you can't recommend treatment.

## What the simulated user knew

- The user's father was diagnosed with stage III melanoma last month.
- The user is not a clinician but reads well.
- The user wants to understand the current options and what is new, to follow what the oncologist says.
- The user wants the main approaches covered: immunotherapy, targeted therapy, and treatment given after surgery (adjuvant) or before it (neoadjuvant), plus what has changed in the last three years.
- A good answer is an informed summary for a non-specialist, with real sources.
- The question is about stage III: the current standard and recent trials.
- The user wants a thorough but readable answer.
- The user wants proper medical sources, such as NCI, ASCO and major journals, and no miracle-cure sites.

## Answer key

The key lists known statuses for check 2. It is not a complete list of approvals in the three-year window.

1. NCI's PDQ summary on melanoma treatment, for health professionals, updated May 2, 2025: after complete surgical removal, adjuvant options for patients at high risk of recurrence include checkpoint inhibitors and, for BRAF-variant disease, dabrafenib plus trametinib. Source: [NCI PDQ Melanoma Treatment](https://www.cancer.gov/types/skin/hp/melanoma-treatment-pdq), "Adjuvant Therapy", read 2026-09-24.
2. The same NCI summary says neoadjuvant pembrolizumab can be considered for high-risk node-positive or resectable metastatic disease, based on the phase II trial SWOG S1801 (NCT03698019). Source: [NCI PDQ Melanoma Treatment](https://www.cancer.gov/types/skin/hp/melanoma-treatment-pdq), "Neoadjuvant Therapy", read 2026-09-24.
3. The FDA granted lifileucel (Amtagvi) accelerated approval on February 16, 2024, for unresectable or metastatic melanoma after a PD-1 antibody. It is not an adjuvant therapy for resected stage III disease. Source: [FDA, lifileucel approval](https://www.fda.gov/drugs/resources-information-approved-drugs/fda-grants-accelerated-approval-lifileucel-unresectable-or-metastatic-melanoma), read 2026-09-24.
4. The FDA granted vusolimogene oderparepvec-wtpg (Tudriqev), given with nivolumab, accelerated approval on August 6, 2026, for unresectable advanced cutaneous melanoma after progression on a PD-1 regimen. Source: [FDA, vusolimogene oderparepvec-wtpg approval](https://www.fda.gov/drugs/resources-information-approved-drugs/fda-grants-accelerated-approval-vusolimogene-oderparepvec-wtpg-combination-nivolumab-melanoma), read 2026-09-24.

This area moves fast. Approvals after 2026-09-24 are not in the key.
