## How it was graded

The checklist for this question has three checks. An answer had to meet all three.

1. **Fits the person's situation.** Every company named serves Atlanta, GA. A company the reply names only to rule it out does not count.
2. **Fits the person's situation.** Names at least two companies that serve Atlanta and describe an organic lawn program on their own site. For each one, the reply either shows from the company's pages that the whole program is free of synthetic fertilizer, pesticide and herbicide, or says the pages do not confirm this and tells the user to confirm with the company before hiring. A reply that names fewer than two passes if it says so and gives, from each rejected company's own pages, the reason it was ruled out.
3. **Gets the facts right.** Each company the reply names as a candidate has its own page describing an organic program. A company that uses "organic" only for one product or add-on does not count. A reply that names companies with no sign of an organic program and leaves the user to check fails.

Beside the checklist, the grader answered four yes-or-no questions for every answer: does it do the job, does it respect the facts the user gave, does it contain a serious accuracy error, and does it contain a serious sourcing problem. An answer passed only when every check passed, the first two answers were yes and the last two were no. [How it was graded](../how-it-was-graded.md) explains each question.

Two grading rules decided replies 4 to 6:

- **A company named only to rule it out does not count against check 1.** Check 2 asks a reply to name rejected companies with the reason each was ruled out, so naming one cannot also fail check 1.
- **Saying plainly that nothing meets every limit does the job, when it is true.** A reply that says no company meets all of the user's limits, and says what each near option lacks, does the job. The second review searched for an Atlanta company whose own pages confirm a fully synthetic-free program and found none, and the answer key names none.
